Executive Summary
This report presents the findings of a controlled phishing simulation conducted using GoPhish to assess participants’ awareness of phishing emails, their ability to identify suspicious links, and their knowledge of incident reporting procedures. A total of 10 simulated emails were sent, providing an opportunity to evaluate how participants interacted with the email and the associated landing page.The results revealed significant gaps in phishing awareness and email security practices. Of the 10 emails sent, 8 were opened, representing an 80% open rate. Furthermore, 6 participants clicked the simulated phishing link, resulting in a 60% click rate, while the same number visited the landing page. Only 2 participants reported the simulated email, representing a 20% reporting rate. No credential submissions were recorded.
The findings suggest that participants demonstrate limited caution when interacting with unexpected emails, particularly when assessing suspicious links and determining whether an email should be reported. Although no credentials were submitted, the high click rate indicates a potential exposure to phishing-related threats. The absence of credential submissions is a positive outcome, but it does not eliminate the underlying awareness gaps.
Overall, the simulation highlights the need to strengthen the organization's human-factor security controls through continuous cybersecurity awareness training, practical phishing recognition exercises, clearly defined reporting procedures, and periodic assessments. Implementing these measures will help improve users' ability to recognize, avoid, and report phishing attempts, thereby reducing the organization's exposure to social engineering attacks.
